Hardware

  • FPGA neural network accelerator: A 784→30→30→10→10→10 network in Verilog and SystemVerilog, from PyTorch training through Q2.13 quantization to synthesis on a Lattice ECP5-85K. 95.24% accuracy, bit-exact with software across 10,000 images, 22.34 MHz Fmax and about 24,800 inferences per second. Found and fixed 8 hardware and numerical bugs on the way.
  • QubeSat FPGA: The timing core for the QubeSat payload on an Artix-7. A time-to-digital converter built from 192 taps of the fast carry chain timestamps photon arrivals at about 22 ps per tap, with code-density calibration. A clock-recovery loop (NCO, phase detector, noise gate and gear-shifted PI loop) then locks onto the satellite's clock from sparse, jittery photon hits, verified bit-exact against a fixed-point model. The multi-channel version runs 4 quantum channels plus sync on one time base.
  • STM32 development board: A two-layer STM32F103C8T6 board designed from scratch in KiCad 10: USB Micro-B input, AMS1117-3.3 LDO, 16 MHz crystal with sized load caps, per-pin decoupling, ferrite-filtered VDDA, and SWD, UART and I2C headers. All 32 parts placed against JLCPCB design rules.
  • Closed-loop vacuum process controller: STM32 pressure controller with PI control and anti-windup, split-range pump and bleed-valve actuation, interlocks and telemetry.
  • Hack computer: Von Neumann computer with CPU, RAM, ROM and I/O, built through Nand2Tetris.

Research

  • Ember: An extensible benchmark suite for quantum annealing embedding algorithms. Published and presented at IEEE Quantum Week 2026.
  • Radiation effects research: Undergraduate researcher in Dr. Bharat Bhuva's group, part of Vanderbilt's Radiation Effects and Reliability program, the largest university-based radiation-effects program in the world.
  • Information-optimal photonic test structures: Sidewall angle is the hardest silicon-photonics fabrication error to measure without cutting the wafer. I treated choosing test structures as an experiment-design problem: a full-vector mode solver gives each structure's sensitivity to width, thickness and sidewall angle, and a Fisher information matrix with its Cramér-Rao bound says how well those can be recovered. Today's structures sit on an almost flat 2-D sheet of that space; adding rib waveguides points into the empty direction, improving the worst-determined parameter about 570x and sidewall-angle precision 23 to 51x.
  • Gallium nitride for high-power semiconductors: Published first-author review on GaN as a replacement for silicon in high-power devices.
